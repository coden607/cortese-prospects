Subject: The tavern that invented "pitza" — never busy again

Hi Senape's team,

835 N Vine Street — where a sign-painter's misspelling in 1933 gave Hazleton the word "pitza," and where families have been calling in orders for nearly a century. That history is your superpower, and the phone line is its front door: when the tavern's full and the line's tied up, a caller who hears a busy signal just orders from the next place on Vine. You'd never know what slipped away.

We built a missed-call text-back for exactly this: busy line fires an instant text to the caller — "Senape's here! Sorry we missed you — text your order?" — onto a counter tablet by the bar. Your crew confirms like any ticket; we log the recovered sale and bill 15% of it. $0 upfront, no new number, no equipment to buy. Slow week? Zero owed.

I'd love to demo it live with a Hazleton number — two minutes whenever the bar's quiet.
