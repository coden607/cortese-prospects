# OUTREACH.md — Sales kit: Continuity OS missed-call recovery for restaurants

## The offer (one paragraph)

When your phone rings during Friday dinner rush and you're elbow-deep in orders, that call goes to a busy signal — and the caller dials your competitor. We fix that: your carrier forwards busy/no-answer calls to us, the customer instantly gets a text back from your restaurant's number ("Sorry we missed you — order here or reply and we'll call you right back"), and the order lands on a counter tablet your staff works like a normal ticket. There's no upfront cost, no monthly fee, and no new hardware to buy — we only earn **15% of the sales we actually recover**, invoiced monthly from the tablet's order log. If we recover nothing, you pay nothing.

**Owner:** Stephen · **Territory:** Broome County NY + I-81/I-86/I-88 corridor (Syracuse–Utica–Elmira) + NEPA (Sayre/Scranton/Wilkes-Barre)

## Cold-call script (owners)

**Call window: 2:00–4:00 PM only. Never lunch (11–1) or dinner (5–8) rush.** Ask for the owner by name; if they're cooking, ask when to call back — do not pitch a line cook.

> "Hi [name], this is Stephen, I'm local over in Binghamton. I'll be quick — I call restaurants for one reason: I tried to order from you last Friday around six and your line was busy. I know, I know, that's the good problem. Here's why I actually called: I run a service that turns those busy signals into texted-back orders on a tablet at your counter. You pay nothing up front — we take a small cut of orders we recover, and only orders we recover. Could I drop by Tuesday around 3 for ten minutes? I'll bring your own Friday call log so you can see it with your own eyes."

Then shut up. The pause does the selling. The drop-by ask is the only goal of call #1.

**If they say "send me info":** text them two things — the busy-signal screenshot from your demo (below) and one line: *"15% of recovered orders only. $0 upfront. Ten minutes Tuesday at 3?"* Do not email essays.

**Call cadence:** Day 1 call → Day 3 text with screenshot → Day 7 final call → park for 90 days. Log every touch in the CRM sheet. Mark any "stop calling" instantly and permanently.

## The busy-signal demo tactic (highest-converting move)

1. **Friday, 5:30–6:30 PM:** call the prospect's published line from your phone. If it rings out or hits busy, screenshot the call log (iOS/Android both show "call failed" / busy).
2. If it *doesn't* hit busy, that's intel too — try again the next Friday. Do not fake it; the tactic only works because it's real.
3. **Open with it:** *"I tried to give you money Friday at 6:14 and couldn't get through. Here's the screenshot. That's not me complaining — that's the exact thing I fix."*
4. Show the screenshot, then show a 30-second screen recording of the recovery flow: busy → text-back → tablet ticket. Offer the 2-week pilot on the spot.
5. For multi-line shops, ask the owner: *"How many lines do you run at rush? When both are lit, where do those calls go?"* The answer is always "nowhere," and they know it.

**Legal/etiquette line:** one demo call is research, not harassment. Never auto-dial, never spoof, never call during rush repeatedly. One attempt, evidence saved, move on.

## Objection handling

**"We already have online ordering."**
"Great — that means your customers already order digitally. The ones hitting a busy signal at 6 PM aren't on your website; they're on the phone because it's Friday and they're tired. We catch the people your online ordering never sees: the caller, not the clicker. And we put those orders on the same tablet, so your staff doesn't learn anything new."

**"Why would I give you 15% of my sales?"**
"Not your sales — recovered sales. Orders that today equal exactly zero because the customer hit a busy signal and called [competitor]. Zero-dollars orders are worth fifteen cents on the dollar to you, every time. And the invoice only ever comes from the tablet's order log — you audit every line."

**"What if it breaks / stops working?"**
"Two answers. First, if the system is down, your phone just behaves exactly like it does today — nothing lost, nothing changes. Second: you don't pay when we don't recover. A broken system costs you nothing by design. That's the whole point of no-upfront."

**"We're corporate-owned / I have to ask the franchise."**
"Perfect — that's actually the best version of this. One pilot location, two weeks, and you walk into your next ops meeting with a recovered-revenue report from a real store. Who do I talk to at corporate, and can I use you as the internal champion?" (For true franchised groups like Grotto/Pontillo's: pitch the local franchisee first, then offer to co-pitch the area developer.)

**"I don't want another tablet / another app."**
"Understood — your counter is crowded. Options: use the tablet we provision (takes one outlet), or we push recovered orders to your existing POS printer if it supports email printing. Either way, staff approve orders with one tap; there's no keyboard work."

**"Is this a robot answering my phone?"**
"No — the customer hears your normal ringing. If you pick up, nothing changes. We only act when the call *doesn't* get answered: instant text-back from your number with your menu link, plus the order ticket if they order from the text. Humans keep the phone; we mop up the misses."

**"How do I know your numbers are real?"**
"You don't have to trust them — the tablet shows every recovered order with timestamp, phone number, and total. Your money, your audit. I expect you to check."

## Monthly billing mechanic

1. **The tablet is the ledger.** Every recovered order is logged with timestamp, caller number, items, and total. The owner can open the tablet (or a web dashboard) and see the same log we see.
2. **Invoice on the 1st, for last month's recovered orders only:** line-item summary (order count, gross recovered, 15% fee) generated from the tablet log. Attach the full CSV if they want it.
3. **Stripe payment link on the invoice.** One click, card on file, done. No checks to chase, no Net-30 arguments.
4. **Auto-reconciled disputes:** if the owner flags an order ("that was a walk-in"), we credit it on the next invoice without a fight. Losing one disputed $4 fee is cheaper than losing a customer.
5. **The invoice is also the retention tool:** pair it with a one-page "recovered revenue report" — *"In March, we recovered $3,214 you would have lost. That's $482 in fees. Here's the call log."* This document is why they renew (see PLAYBOOK.md).
